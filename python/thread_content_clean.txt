- **Subject:** Re: URGENT: Admin access and shared code

________________________________________

Google Ads treats "product linking" as a high-stakes administrative action
because it fundamentally alters the account's capabilities and data access.
Linking services like YouTube to Google Ads has significant consequences:

1. Data Sharing: It permits the flow of potentially sensitive data between
platforms (e.g., YouTube analytics to Ads).
2. Permissions & Control: It grants Google Ads specific rights to access
and use features or data from the linked service (e.g., using video view
data to build remarketing audiences).
3. Advertising Impact: Linked data directly powers critical advertising
functions such as audience targeting, ad creative selection, and conversion
measurement.

These actions affect data privacy, ad performance, and budget utilization.
Requiring "Admin" access ensures that only users with full authority and
accountability can make these crucial decisions, safeguarding the account.

Therefore, while CPH shared codes make initiating a video link much
smoother by using a creator-generated code, they do not solve the
fundamental problem: the user finalizing the link within the Google Ads
interface still needs Admin permissions on the Google Ads account. The
shared code simplifies the request, but the acceptance and establishment of
the link remain an Admin-level function.

Hi Reto,

I know you are already familiar with most of the background here, but I
wanted to share a detailed breakdown of the exact risk dynamics today vs.
tomorrow:

TL;DR: The creator can block specific campaigns from a linked CID (versus
globally blocking all ads). They can already identify advertisers from
YouTube paid views analytics (if only one CID is boosting the video), and
tomorrow they will have access to full performance stats (earned views,
subscribers, etc.) once the transparency and boost tab features launch.
This helps clarify why keeping the Admin permission requirement is
important to manage this level of exposure.

Here is the detailed risk breakdown today vs. tomorrow:
1. The Risks Today

   - View Exposure & Loss of Anonymity: Even without a link, YouTube
   analytics shows creators the total paid views on their videos (though not
   segmented by CID). If only a single CID is running ads, they effectively
   see all traffic from that specific CID. Establishing a link fully reveals
   the advertiser's exact CID and name.
   - Audience List Leakage: If the Ads account is in an open Manager
   Account (MCC) with list sharing enabled, remarketing lists built from the
   video views could leak to other accounts in the MCC.
   - Unilateral Campaign Blocks: Without a link, the creator can use Video
   Ad Control (VidAc) in YouTube Studio to forbid Google Ads usage globally
   across all CIDs. Linking acts as a targeted whitelist entry to bypass this
   block. If the creator breaks the link, the whitelist is revoked, which
   immediately halts campaigns and freezes remarketing audiences.

2. The Risks Tomorrow (Parallel Initiatives)

We are currently building two parallel projects that will change these
dynamics:

   1. The Boost Hub (specifically the Brand Boost tab): A new YouTube
   Studio dashboard surfacing active brand-led campaigns and aggregate
   performance metrics directly to creators.
   2. Creator Ad Controls 2.0 (Ads Performance Transparency workstream): A
   permissions redesign that will let creators allow/block specific CIDs, set
   contract-aware link expirations, and decouple metric sharing from ad reuse
   (boosting) rights.

Added Risks:

   - These features will expose advertiser-side performance metrics (like
   earned views, conversions, and follow-on subscribers) of unlinked
   advertisers, and establishing a link will act as explicit consent for this
   exposure.

Based on this, I now fully understand why requiring Admin access is
important for YouTube linking.

Do you agree?

Olivier
